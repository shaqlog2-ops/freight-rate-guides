### Altamira (ATM)

Every origin tier prices **identically to Veracruz**: USD 8,240 / 8,180 for the 12 main China ports, with the same +50 (20GP) origin premiums up the tier ladder. COSCO publishes one number for both Mexican Gulf gateways in this tariff cycle.

**Key structural facts:**

- **40GP is USD 60 cheaper than 20GP at every single origin** (8,180 vs. 8,240 from main ports). This inversion also appears in COSCO's Panama pricing (40GP USD 240 under 20GP to Colón Free Zone) and has persisted for three consecutive Caribbean tariff vintages.
- No 40HQ is published on this lane in the current tariff — 40GP is the high-volume workhorse for Gulf bookings.
- FAF (fuel adjustment factor) is included in the published number for this tariff family; no separate bunker add-on is listed in the rate sheet.

<a name="why-same"></a>
## 3. Why Veracruz and Altamira Cost Exactly the Same

COSCO prices both Gulf gateways identically because both are reached the same way: a trans-Pacific leg, then transit through the Panama Canal to the Gulf of Mexico. The ocean freight difference between the two ports is **USD 0** — so the port decision is purely an inland-logistics question:

| Your cargo is headed to... | Better gateway | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mexico City, Puebla, Toluca, Bajío (Querétaro, León) | **Veracruz** | Shorter truck/rail lift to central Mexico; historic customs hub with deep broker capacity |
| Monterrey, Saltillo, Reynosa, northern industrial corridor | **Altamira** | Closest Gulf port to Monterrey (~500 km); strong steel/automotive cargo base |
| Tampico / Tuxpan hinterland | Either | Marginal inland distance difference |

This mirrors what we documented on Mexico's Pacific coast in September (Manzanillo = Lázaro Cárdenas = Ensenada, one published price) and again across Peru's Chancay vs. Callao: **on FAK tariffs, port choice inside a country's service loop is an inland-cost decision, not an ocean-freight decision.**

<a name="cheapest"></a>
## 6. Cheapest Way to Ship a Container to Mexico's Gulf

1. **Ship a 40GP, not a 20GP.** At 8,180 vs. 8,240, the 40GP is USD 60 cheaper *and* holds roughly twice the volume. Unless your cargo is dense enough to hit the 20GP payload limit (~26–28 t), a 40GP is strictly better value on this lane right now.
2. **Book in the Nov 8–14 window, not late October.** The tariff reset cut USD 420–440 per box; a one-week departure slip on non-urgent cargo captured a 5% saving.
3. **Origin matters less than you think — but Fuzhou/Lianyungang pay +50–100.** Ten of the twelve main China ports share the base price. If your supplier is flexible on loading port, Shanghai/Ningbo/Shenzhen are the flat-rate doors.
4. **Compare against the Pacific route honestly.** Our Sep Pacific tariff published 7,000 (20GP) / 7,300 (40GP+40HQ) to Manzanillo/Lázaro Cárdenas/Ensenada — different vintage, but the structural Gulf premium over the Pacific is real (third-party guides peg it at USD 500–700 per box on spot markets). If your inland destination is Monterrey or the north, Gulf usually wins door-to-door; for Guadalajara or the west, Pacific almost always wins.
5. **If your cargo can move as LCL and is under ~13–15 CBM**, third-party market references put China→Mexico LCL at roughly USD 150–250 per CBM — below that volume, LCL can beat the 20GP. Our database carries no live COSCO LCL file for the Gulf this cycle, so we cite market ranges, not verified rates. Break-even math: at 8,240 for a 20GP (~28 CBM usable), FCL beats LCL above roughly 15 CBM.

<a name="tariff-reform"></a>
## 7. Mexico's 2026 Tariff Reform: What Chinese Goods Pay

The ocean freight above is only the freight. Since **January 1, 2026**, Mexico's General Import and Export Tax Law imposes a permanent tariff increase on non-FTA origin countries — including China — covering **1,463 product categories with rates of 10–50%** across roughly 17 industries. An **April 2026 extension added 185 more categories at 5–35%**, effective indefinitely. Third-party trade guides summarize the headline bands:

| Industry | Duty band (2026 reform) |
|---|---|
| Textiles & apparel | 10–35% (1,014 categories) |
| Steel & iron | 15–50% (249 categories) |
| Automotive & parts | 20–50% (235 categories) |
| Plastics | 10–35% |
| Footwear/leather | 10–30% |
| Furniture | 15–35% |
| Appliances | 15–30% |

USMCA/EU-origin goods are exempt — which is why some importers route Chinese components through US transloading (the 20–30-day land-bridge option above). Practical consequences for Chinese shippers: verify your HS code against the 2026 schedule **before** booking, because misclassification is a leading cause of customs holds in VUCEM (Mexico's single-window system), and confirm your consignee holds an RFC tax ID and active **Padrón de Importadores** registration (plus sector padrón for textiles, footwear and steel).

*(Tariff-reform figures are from Mexico's published tax-law changes as reported by third-party trade guides; they are not SHAQ-verified rates. Duty computation is cargo-specific — treat as planning context.)*

<a name="landed-cost"></a>
## 8. Landed-Cost Stack: Duties, IVA, DTA and Destination Charges

On top of the USD 8,240/8,180 ocean freight, a Veracruz/Altamira import carries (third-party market references, Oct–Nov 2026):

| Cost item | Reference range | Note |
|---|---|---|
| IGI import duty | 0–50% of CIF, by HS code | 2026 reform bands above; IMMEX programs can defer |
| IVA (VAT) | **16%** on CIF + duty | "Tax on tax" — often the largest landed line |
| DTA customs processing fee | ~0.8% of customs value | Mexican customs law requirement |
| Precompetencia fee | Minor, statutory | Added to DTA line |
| Origin charges (China) | USD 150–300 | THC, documentation, export handling |
| Destination charges (Mexico) | USD 250–500 | Port handling, terminal, docs |
| Licensed agente aduanal | Mandatory, broker-quoted | Only a licensed Mexican broker can file the pedimento |
| DDP door-to-door (market) | ~USD 6,500–8,500 per box (Veracruz/Altamira, mid-2026 spot) | Includes duty, IVA, clearance, inland delivery |

The pattern we keep seeing across Latin American lanes: **destination-side costs (duty + 16% IVA + broker + handling) routinely exceed the ocean freight itself** on Chinese-origin cargo under the 2026 tariff regime. Budget the full stack, not the freight number.

## FAQ

**Q1: What does a 40ft container cost from China to Veracruz right now?**
USD 8,180 per 40GP port-to-port from 12 main Chinese ports under COSCO's published FAK for Nov 8–14, 2026. A 20GP costs USD 8,240 — USD 60 *more* than the 40GP.

**Q2: Is Veracruz or Altamira cheaper to ship to?**
Neither — COSCO publishes identical FAK rates to both (8,240/8,180). The difference is inland: Veracruz feeds central Mexico (Mexico City, Bajío), Altamira feeds Monterrey and the northern corridor. Choose by final delivery point, not ocean rate.

**Q3: How long does shipping from China to Mexico's Gulf coast take?**
Third-party market guides put Gulf port-to-port transits at 32–45 days via the Panama Canal, versus 18–28 days to Mexico's Pacific ports on direct services. Add 3–5 days for customs release and inland delivery.

**Q4: Why is a 40GP cheaper than a 20GP to Mexico?**
Carrier pricing structure, not an error: COSCO's Caribbean tariff has priced 40s below 20s for three consecutive vintages (−USD 60 to Mexico Gulf, −USD 240 to Panama). It signals carriers want volume utilization on the larger box — importers with stackable cargo should simply take it.

**Q5: What duties will my goods pay in Mexico in 2026?**
Under the Jan 1, 2026 reform, 1,463 Chinese-origin product categories carry 10–50% duties (textiles 10–35%, steel 15–50%, auto parts 20–50%), plus 16% IVA on CIF+duty and ~0.8% DTA. Check your exact HS code; USMCA/EU-origin goods are exempt.

**Q6: What is the cheapest way to ship from China to Mexico?**
For full containers: book a 40GP in the November tariff window (USD 8,180, down 5.4% week-over-week) and pick the coast that matches your inland destination. Under ~15 CBM, LCL (market references USD 150–250/CBM) may beat the 20GP. For Monterrey/north, the Gulf door beats Pacific+inland; for Guadalajara/west, the Pacific door usually wins.

**Q7: Is the COSCO FAK rate the final price I pay?**
No. It is the port-to-port ocean freight with FAF included. Add origin charges (USD 150–300), Mexican duty/IGI, 16% IVA, DTA, destination charges (USD 250–500) and the mandatory agente aduanal fee. A mid-2026 market DDP door-to-door reference for Veracruz/Altamira runs about USD 6,500–8,500 including taxes and delivery.
