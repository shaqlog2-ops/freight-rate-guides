## Why three Pacific ports cost the same

Mexico's three main container gateways on the Pacific — **Manzanillo (Colima)**, **Lázaro Cárdenas (Michoacán)**, and **Ensenada (Baja California)** — are all served by COSCO's AE7/AC7 (or equivalent) trans-Pacific services that call the West Coast of Mexico on a single rotation, with transshipment via a Mexican Pacific hub port (typically Manzanillo or Lázaro Cárdenas).

Because the vessel rotation, bunker cost, port handling cost, and route distance are the same per the September 14, 2026 COSCO rate sheet, **the same number is filed for all three Mexican ports across all 50 origin ports loaded into our rate database.** This is unusual in market practice — most carrier tariffs show small variations between Mexican Pacific ports — but it's consistent across the current 50 origin × 4 destination COSCO matrix.

The practical consequence:

- If you only care about base ocean freight — pick the port closest to your final inland destination.
- If you ship to the U.S. West Coast as well, **Ensenada** doubles as a Mexico-U.S. Southwest cross-border option (Tijuana / San Diego).
- If you ship to central Mexico (Mexico City, Guadalajara, Querétaro, Puebla) — **Manzanillo** has the densest inland rail and trucking network.
- If you ship to the Bajío manufacturing cluster (León, Silao, Aguascalientes, San Luis Potosí) or to northern Mexico via a shorter cross-mountain corridor — **Lázaro Cárdenas** has growing intermodal capacity.

## How to choose the right Mexico port

| Final inland destination (typical) | Best Mexico Pacific port | Why |
|-----------------------------------|--------------------------|-----|
| Mexico City, Guadalajara, Puebla | **Manzanillo** | Direct rail and highway to central Mexico; most-established intermodal hub |
| Bajío (León, Silao, Aguascalientes), western Mexico | **Lázaro Cárdenas** | Newer intermodal terminal, lower congestion, growing rail links |
| Tijuana, Mexicali, U.S. Southwest cross-dock | **Ensenada** | Closest to U.S. border at Tijuana; ideal for cross-border operations |
| Monterrey, Saltillo, central Mexico industrial belt | **Altamira (Gulf)** | Direct to Monterrey without Pacific crossing; USD 1,800 / 20GP premium reflects trans-Mexico routing |

**Lázaro Cárdenas rail advantage:** Lázaro Cárdenas is positioned about 7 hours by rail to Mexico City, vs Manzanillo's roughly 10 hours — Lázaro Cárdenas can be the faster door-to-door option for central Mexico industrial destinations like Querétaro / San Luis Potosí / northern Bajío despite identical base ocean rates, because the shorter overland leg offsets any cross-coast routing inefficiency. Published Dantful port-comparison data confirms Lázaro Cárdenas → Mexico City transit is shorter than Manzanillo → Mexico City, which matters for time-sensitive cargo.

For most general shippers with no specific inland preference, **Manzanillo is the default recommendation** because it offers the most inland transport options and the highest weekly sailing frequency.

**Note on new 2026 direct services:** MSC launched a new direct weekly route from Tianjin to Mexico's west coast in July 2026 with 5,000-TEU vessels, achieving 15–16 days Tianjin → Manzanillo / Lázaro Cárdenas / Ensenada and bypassing Korean transshipment. COSCO and other OA carriers continue to offer 17–22 day south-China → Pacific Mexico direct call options. Capacity for north-China shippers (Tianjin, Qingdao, Dalian) has materially improved in 2026 as a result.

## Altamira vs. the Pacific trio — when to pay USD 1,800 more

**Altamira** sits on Mexico's Gulf coast in the state of Tamaulipas. COSCO serves Altamira via an Asia–Mexico east-coast loop that includes U.S. Gulf and Panama Canal–transit routing, hence the ~USD 1,800 premium per 20GP.

The premium is justified when:

- **Final inland destination is northern or central Mexico industrial belt** — Monterrey (Nuevo León), Saltillo, San Luis Potosí, Querétaro via a Gulf-coast entry avoiding the Pacific crossing.
- **Goods avoid the Sea of Cortez / west-coast trucking** — fragile, high-value, time-sensitive cargo for which inland transit risk is better taken via a shorter cross-mexico route from Altamira.
- **Cargo on the same B/L needs dual-coast Mexican distribution** — common for retail and consumer-electronics importers with stores on both coasts.

If your final destination is **Mexico City, Guadalajara, or the Bajío** — staying on the Pacific trio saves USD 1,800 / 20GP.

## What the USD 7,000 / 20GP rate does NOT include

The published tariff rate is **base ocean freight only**. A complete delivered cost will add most or all of the following categories; specific values vary by origin, destination, and seasonality:

| Cost category | Who charges | Typical range / how it works |
|---------------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| **Origin Terminal Handling Charge (OTHC)** | Origin terminal | Origin THC + doc fee + seal fee vary by Chinese port (e.g. Shanghai, Ningbo, Yantian each publish separate tariffs) |
| **Bunker Adjustment Factor (BAF)** | Carrier (surcharge) | Adjusts monthly with oil price; can add 5–15% on top of base |
| **Panama Canal / transit surcharge** | Carrier or canal authority | Currently suspended/normalized through 2026; may re-apply with vessel booking system changes |
| **Document fee / Telex release** | Origin office | USD 25–75 per B/L typical |
| **VGM (Verified Gross Mass)** | Origin terminal | Required for SOLAS compliance; usually a small per-container fee |
| **Origin customs clearance (China export)** | Chinese broker | RMB 200–500 per shipment for standard HS codes |
| **Destination Terminal Handling Charge (DTHC)** | Mexican terminal | Manzanillo / Lázaro / Ensenada / Altamira each publish their own DTHC schedule |
| **Mexican customs broker (pedimento)** | Mexican agent | MXP 4,500–9,000 per pedimento (single B/L) |
| **Duty + DTA + IVA (Mexico import tax)** | SAT (Mexican tax authority) | Mexico's January 2026 tariff update expanded duties 5–50% on 1,463+ tariff lines for non-FTA origins — see [Mexico customs context](#mexico-customs-context) below |
| **Demurrage / free time** | Mexican terminal | Carrier free time 7–14 days typical; demurrage begins after, escalating daily |
| **DDP (door delivery) trucking** | Mexican trucking | Long-haul trucking from Manzanillo to Mexico City USD 1,500–2,500 per 40HQ |

## Mexico customs context

Mexico's import regime changed materially in **2025–2026**:

- **January 2026 tariff law** — Mexico's federal government expanded tariffs on **1,463 tariff lines** to **5–50%** for goods originating in non-FTA countries (which includes most Chinese-origin goods not covered by an early-2026 bilateral arrangement). Sectors most affected: textiles and apparel (typical 25–35%), footwear (35%), steel and aluminum (50%), home appliances (10–15%), and automotive parts (10–20%).
- **ANAM (Agencia Nacional de Aduanas de México)** now performs stricter HS-code audits and valuation reviews on inbound containers from Asia, especially on first-time importers and on repeat shipments with the same HS code from the same supplier.
- **IVA (value-added tax)** remains 16% federally (with state-level adjustments bringing it to ~16.5% in some border zones).
- **Advance electronic pedimento** is required — your Mexican customs broker must file the pedimento before vessel arrival; same-day customs clearance at Manzanillo and Lázaro Cárdenas is achievable for non-inspected cargo.
- **Pre-validation programs (OEA / NEEC)** — Authorized Economic Operator status reduces inspection frequency and accelerates clearance.

**Practical consequence for shippers**: For a standard 20GP of Chinese textiles or consumer goods cleared in Mexico, the federal duty under the January 2026 regime is materially higher than it was in 2024. Always request a binding landed-cost quote that includes the SAT duty assessment before booking.

## FAQ

### Q1. What is the cheapest port in Mexico to receive a 20GP container from China?

For base ocean freight as of September 14, 2026, **Manzanillo, Lázaro Cárdenas, and Ensenada are tied at USD 7,000 / 20GP** from major Chinese loading ports (Shanghai, Ningbo, Shenzhen, Hong Kong, Qingdao, Dalian, Xiamen, Busan, Xingang). The cheapest port is therefore the one closest to your final Mexican inland destination — see the decision table in [section 2](#how-to-choose-the-right-mexico-port).

### Q2. Should I ship to Manzanillo or Lázaro Cárdenas?

Same base rate, so pick by **schedule, inland destination, and intermodal capacity**:

- **Manzanillo** for cargo headed to Mexico City, Guadalajara, or central Mexico via rail.
- **Lázaro Cárdenas** for cargo headed to the Bajío (León / Silao / Aguascalientes) or to northern Mexico via a more direct overland corridor; it has newer terminal capacity and lower congestion than Manzanillo in 2026.
- Either port works if your cargo is moving onward by truck to a coastal destination only.

### Q3. When is Altamira the right choice?

Altamira on Mexico's Gulf coast costs **USD 8,800 / 20GP** — about USD 1,800 more than the Pacific trio — but is shorter to **Monterrey, Saltillo, San Luis Potosí, and central Mexico industrial belt** for cargo arriving from Asia. Choose Altamira when inland trucking distance is dramatically shorter than from a Pacific port, or when cross-coast distribution is needed.

### Q4. What is the average transit time from China to a Mexican Pacific port?

Typical COSCO schedules run **17–22 days** from South China (Shanghai / Ningbo / Yantian / Shekou) to Manzanillo or Ensenada on a direct Pacific call, and **19–24 days** to Lázaro Cárdenas (often via Manzanillo transshipment). Altamira via the Panama Canal routing takes **28–34 days**. From North China (Qingdao, Dalian, Xingang) add 2–4 days. Always confirm exact ETD/ETA at booking — vessel rotations shift ±3 days due to weather and Panama Canal transit slot availability.

### Q5. Which Chinese port has the cheapest base freight to Mexico?

The 10 Chinese and nearby Asian ports are **all priced identically at USD 7,000 / 20GP** to Mexico's three Pacific ports in the September 14, 2026 COSCO tariff: Shanghai, Ningbo, Yantian, Shekou, Hong Kong, Qingdao, Dalian, Busan, Xiamen, Xingang. Pick the closest to your supplier to minimize inland trucking in China.

### Q6. Is LCL cheaper than FCL for shipping from China to Mexico?

Not below ~13–15 CBM. Below that breakpoint, LCL per-CBM × cargo volume usually beats a full container cost. Above the breakpoint, FCL wins because you avoid per-CBM LCL handling fees and there is less consolidation handling. For shipments under 8 CBM, our consolidation desk can quote LCL to Mexican Pacific ports on request.

### Q7. What 2026 Mexico tariff changes affect Chinese-origin imports?

Mexico's **January 2026 tariff law** raised import duties to **5–50% on 1,463+ tariff lines** for non-FTA-origin goods (which includes most Chinese-origin cargo). Sectors hit hardest: textiles/apparel (25–35%), footwear (35%), steel and aluminum (50%), home appliances (10–15%), and certain auto parts (10–20%). Customs broker (pedimento) fees and IVA (16% federal VAT) apply separately. Always request a binding SAT duty calculation before shipping.

### Q8. Do I need a Mexican customs broker to import from China?

**Yes.** All commercial imports into Mexico require a licensed *agente aduanal* to file the electronic pedimento with SAT, validate HS codes, and clear customs. ANAM has tightened audits on Asia-origin cargo in 2026, so choosing an experienced broker who knows your HS code is more important than ever. SHAQ Logistics can introduce you to Manzanillo- and Lázaro Cárdenas-based brokers who handle weekly China consolidation.

### Q9. What Mexico port is best for cross-border U.S. distribution?

**Ensenada** — it is the closest Mexican Pacific port to the **Tijuana / San Diego** border crossing, which is the primary gateway for cross-dock operations serving Southern California, Arizona, and Nevada. While Manzanillo also serves the same border corridor, Ensenada saves 24–36 hours of inland transit for cross-border operations.

### Q10. Does the USD 7,000 rate include trucking to my final destination?

**No.** The COSCO base ocean freight rate covers ocean transport only between the named loading port and the named Mexican port. Inland trucking from Manzanillo / Lázaro Cárdenas / Ensenada to Mexico City (or wherever your consignee is) is quoted separately — typically USD 1,500–2,500 per 40HQ for the Manzanillo → Mexico City long-haul. Request an all-in landed quote from the SHAQ Rate Desk.
