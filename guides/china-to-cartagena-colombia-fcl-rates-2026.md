## 5. Cheapest Way to Ship a Container to Colombia

1. **Use the flat 8-port pricing.** Because COSCO's tariff is origin-agnostic across eight Chinese ports, the cheapest door-to-port combination is the port nearest your supplier's factory — ocean freight is a constant, inland trucking is the variable you control.
2. **20GP vs 40GP: the math favors 40GP for volume.** The 40GP premium is only **USD 200 (2.4%)** while a 40GP holds roughly 2.4× the volume of a 20GP (67–76 CBM vs 28–31 CBM). If your cargo exceeds ~55–58 CBM, 40GP is dramatically cheaper per CBM.
3. **LCL for small shipments.** Our database currently carries no live LCL tariff for Colombia (consolidator rates are quoted per-shipment). As a planning rule from our lane analysis, LCL beats FCL below roughly **13–15 CBM**; above that, a 20GP at USD 8,400 wins.
4. **Book against the October 14 tariff validity.** The tariff is flat across two vintages — if your cargo is ready before mid-October, booking now locks the pre-escalation level.

<a name="port-choice"></a>
## 6. Cartagena vs. Buenaventura: Which Colombian Port?

- **Cartagena** (Caribbean coast) is Colombia's largest container gateway and one of the busiest transshipment hubs in the Caribbean. For cargo destined to Bogotá and inland Colombia via the Caribbean routing, Cartagena is the default.
- **Buenaventura** (Pacific coast) is closer to Cali and western Colombia by land, but COSCO's current published tariff sheet carries **no fresh China-origin Buenaventura rate** — its recent published tariff vintages cover Asian (India/Southeast Asia) origins only. China → Buenaventura moves either via transshipment or is quoted on request.
- Practical rule we apply at SHAQ: **Caribbean-bound and Bogotá-bound cargo goes Cartagena; Cali/western cargo is worth a Buenaventura quote comparison** because inland trucking from Buenaventura can offset a higher ocean rate.

<a name="colombia-customs"></a>
## 7. Colombia Import Costs: DIAN, Duty and 19% IVA

These are public Colombian trade-policy facts (not database rates), included because they dominate the landed-cost conversation:

- **No China–Colombia FTA.** Unlike Chile (FTA since 2005) and Peru (since 2009), Colombia has **no free trade agreement with China** — Chinese goods enter under MFN duty rates, typically 0–20% depending on HS code, with higher peaks on sensitive categories.
- **19% IVA (VAT)** applies on the CIF value plus duty for most imports.
- **DIAN** (Colombia's customs authority) clears via the **VUCE** single-window system; a licensed Colombian customs broker (*declarante*) is required for formal entries.
- Cartagena's port community is well-practiced with Chinese trade lanes; the Cartagena free-trade zone supports bonded warehousing if your buyer needs deferred nationalization.

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: How much does it cost to ship a container from China to Cartagena, Colombia in October 2026?**
A: USD 8,400 for a 20GP and USD 8,600 for a 40GP from Shanghai, Ningbo, Shekou, Hong Kong, Xiamen, Qingdao, Xingang or Dalian on COSCO's tariff valid through October 14, 2026.

**Q2: Is there a 40HQ rate to Cartagena?**
A: The October 2026 published tariff lists only 20GP (USD 8,400) and 40GP (USD 8,600). 40HQ equipment is allocated at booking on request — ask your forwarder to confirm equipment type and any equipment adjustment at confirmation.

**Q3: Why is Cartagena more expensive than Mexico or Peru from China?**
A: In our tracked September–October 2026 tariffs, Mexico Pacific 40GP was USD 7,300, Peru (Chancay/Callao) 40GP USD 5,800, and Cartagena 40GP is USD 8,600. The Cartagena premium reflects Caribbean routing via Panama and fewer weekly direct departures than the Pacific South America services.

**Q4: Does Colombia have a free trade agreement with China?**
A: No. Colombia applies MFN duty rates (commonly 0–20%) to Chinese goods plus 19% IVA. This differs from Chile and Peru, which both have China FTAs — a key landed-cost difference when choosing between Andean markets.

**Q5: Which Chinese port is cheapest for shipping to Colombia?**
A: Eight major ports price identically (USD 8,400/20GP, USD 8,600/40GP). Fuzhou is +USD 50/+100 and Lianyungang +USD 100/+100. The cheapest total cost comes from the port closest to your supplier, since ocean freight is constant.

**Q6: Are rates rising or falling on this lane?**
A: Rising, then flattening: 40GP went from USD 8,100 in late August to USD 8,600 in October (+6.2%), and the two most recent weekly tariff vintages (Oct 7, Oct 14) are identical — a hold, not a further increase.

**Q7: Should I use LCL or FCL for Colombia?**
A: Below roughly 13–15 CBM, LCL consolidation is usually cheaper; above that, a 20GP at USD 8,400 is the better unit cost. Our database currently has no live published LCL tariff for Colombia — request a per-CBM quote for your exact volume.

**Q8: How far in advance should I book?**
A: Standard practice on this lane is 10–14 days before vessel cutoff. The current tariff is valid through October 14, 2026.
