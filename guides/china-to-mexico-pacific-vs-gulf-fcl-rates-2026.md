# Shipping from China to Mexico 2026: Pacific vs. Gulf Coast FCL Rates — Manzanillo USD 5,100 vs. Veracruz USD 8,180 (40GP, Nov 5–14 COSCO Tariff)

**By SHAQ Logistics Rate Desk | Published November 9, 2026 | Rate data: COSCO Guideline FAK tariffs, effective November 5–14, 2026**

**BLUF:** Shipping a 40GP container from Shanghai to Manzanillo on Mexico's Pacific coast currently costs **USD 5,100**, while the same box to Veracruz on the Gulf coast costs **USD 8,180** — a **USD 3,080 gap (+60%)** for landing in the same country. A 20GP shows the same split: **USD 5,250 to Manzanillo vs. USD 8,240 to Veracruz (+57%)**. Pacific rates fell another **7.1% week-over-week** and are now **25–30% below September levels**, while Gulf rates have been flat for three consecutive tariff weeks. The coast you choose is worth more than any negotiation.

| **Gulf premium** | +USD 2,990 (+57%) | +USD 3,080 (+60%) | — | |

Three facts worth pausing on:

- **The Gulf has been flat** at 8,240/8,180 for three consecutive weekly tariff vintages (Oct 30, Nov 2 and Nov 8 files all show the same numbers), while the Pacific keeps sliding.
- **40GP is cheaper than 20GP on both coasts** — USD 150 cheaper on the Pacific, USD 60 cheaper on the Gulf. If your volume fits a 40GP, a 20GP is never the right box this month.
- COSCO's fuel adjustment factor (FAF) is **already included** in all numbers above: +470/20GP and +940/40GP on the Pacific (AAW tariff, since Nov 5) vs. +440/20GP and +880/40GP on the Gulf (ACA tariff, since Nov 8).

## 5. Transit Times & Alternative Modes

COSCO's tariff files do not publish transit times; the following are third-party market references (Transworld / Presou / DTFU 2026 guides), labeled as such:

| Leg | Pacific coast | Gulf coast |
|---|---|---|
| Port-to-port, direct service | ~18–28 days | ~32–45 days (via Panama Canal) |
| Port-to-port, transshipment | ~32–40 days | 40+ days |
| Realistic door-to-door (incl. handling, clearance, inland) | 35–47 days | 43–55 days |

Third-party routing economics: **direct services run about 10–15% above transshipment prices but save roughly a week**; a sea-air hybrid via Los Angeles (sea to LAX, then truck/air into Mexico) is quoted at USD 1,500–2,500 with 14–18 days door-to-door but requires customs handling in two countries.

**The MSC Tianjin direct route matters here.** Since July 2026, MSC has operated a weekly direct service from Tianjin (Xingang) to Mexico's Pacific coast — 7 vessels of ~5,000 TEU, cutting transit to roughly **15–16 days port-to-port** versus 8–12 days longer via Korean transshipment, and adding an estimated 60,000 TEU/year of capacity. Note that our COSCO tariff prices Xingang at the same flat **USD 5,250/5,100** as Shanghai — so a North China shipper on the MSC express service pays the same ocean tariff while saving 1.5–2 weeks versus the south-China origins' longer loops (third-party carrier schedule information).

Demurrage warning (third-party): Mexican holiday peaks and terminal congestion at Manzanillo and Veracruz can add 4–7 days and daily demurrage fees — pre-clear documents with your broker and confirm free time before booking.

<a name="coast-choice"></a>
## 6. Which Coast Should You Ship To?

The USD 3,080 gap is not a "Pacific is always better" signal — it is a routing-structure signal. The Gulf costs more because vessels route via the Panama Canal (32–45 days vs. 18–28 days). Choose by where your cargo lands inland:

| If your destination is... | Better coast | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Guadalajara, León, Bajío manufacturing belt | Pacific (Manzanillo/Lázaro) | Shortest truck/rail leg; Lázaro Cárdenas has the fastest rail link to Guadalajara/Mexico City |
| Mexico City, Puebla, Toluca | Either — compute landed cost | Pacific wins on ocean freight (5,100 vs. 8,180/40GP) but Gulf trucking can be shorter; the 3,080 gap usually wins for Pacific unless inland differential exceeds ~3,000 |
| Monterrey / northern industrial zone | Gulf (Altamira) or US land bridge | Altamira is the nearest deep-water port; USMCA land-bridge routing via Laredo is an alternative |
| Veracruz corridor, Gulf/Yucatán markets | Gulf (Veracruz) | Local delivery economics; Veracruz corridor serves east-central Mexico |

A worked example: 20 pallets (~55 CBM) into a 40GP from Shanghai. Via Manzanillo: USD 5,100 ocean + inland leg. Via Veracruz: USD 8,180 ocean — the **USD 3,080 difference buys roughly 1,000–1,500 km of extra trucking** (third-party market trucking rates ~USD 1.0–1.5/km for FTL in Mexico). Unless your warehouse is closer to the Gulf than to the Pacific rail network, the Pacific wins on landed cost.

<a name="tariff-reform"></a>
## 7. Mexico's 2026 Tariff Reform: The Landed-Cost Context

Freight is only one line of the landed-cost stack. Mexico's 2026 tariff reform (third-party policy facts, attributed): from **January 1, 2026**, Mexico applies permanent duties of **10–50% on 1,463 product categories** from countries without an FTA (China included) across ~17 industries — steel 15–50% (249 categories), automotive 20–50% (235), textiles 10–35% (1,014), plastics 10–35% (81), furniture 15–35% (28). In **April 2026** an additional **185 categories** were extended at 5–35% indefinitely (cosmetics up to 35%, bicycles 35%, auto parts 10–35%). US/Canada/EU-origin goods are exempt.

On top of duty: **IGI** (per HS fraction), **IVA 16%**, **DTA 0.8%** (8 per thousand of customs value), plus mandatory *agente aduanal* (customs broker) fees and NOM standards compliance. A Mexico City door delivery of a USD 5,100-freight 40GP typically lands at **USD 6,500–8,500 all-in** in current DDP market quotes — third-party market reference. Verify your HS code in VUCEM before booking; misclassification is the leading cause of customs holds.

<a name="cheapest"></a>
## 8. The Cheapest Way to Ship from China to Mexico

1. **Book a 40GP, not two 20GPs.** 40GP at 5,100 vs. 2×20GP at 10,500 — **51% cheaper per box pair**, and 40HQ costs the same as 40GP on the Pacific lane (5,100) for +13% internal volume. Per-CBM, a 40HQ's 68 CBM works out to ~USD 75/CBM vs. ~USD 135/CBM in a 20GP.
2. **Ship Pacific unless the inland leg says otherwise** (see the decision table in Section 6).
3. **Use a flat-origin port.** Shanghai/Ningbo/Yantian/Shekou/Xiamen/Qingdao/Dalian all price at the 5,250/5,100 base — paying +120 from a Japanese feeder port buys nothing on this lane.
4. **Watch the FAF cycle.** FAF is +470/940 on the Pacific this week (up from +400/800 in October) — but the underlying base rate fell faster, so the all-in number still dropped. Ask for the base/FAF split on every quote so a rising FAF can't hide behind a "stable" rate.
5. **For ≤15 CBM, price LCL against FCL break-even** — third-party Mexico LCL market rates run USD 78–208/CBM (Presou/Transworld 2026 references; our LCL database has no current Mexico rows). At 78–208 USD/CBM, ~30 CBM already exceeds the 5,100 40GP.
6. **Time the booking to the tariff week.** Both current tariffs expire November 14; the Pacific has moved −400 in each of the last two weeks. If your cargo is ready, don't pre-book before expiry — re-quote on the new vintage.

## 9. FAQ

**Q1: How much does it cost to ship a 40GP container from China to Mexico right now?**
On the published COSCO tariff valid November 5–14, 2026: USD 5,100 to Manzanillo/Lázaro Cárdenas/Ensenada (Pacific) and USD 8,180 to Veracruz/Altamira (Gulf) from Shanghai and 10 other main China ports, FAF included. Spot rates can be lower — request a live quote.

**Q2: Why is the Gulf coast 60% more expensive than the Pacific?**
Gulf vessels route via the Panama Canal (32–45 days port-to-port vs. 18–28 days on the Pacific), so carriers price the longer, slot-constrained routing higher. In COSCO's Caribbean sheet, Gulf Mexico ports sit near the top of the ladder: Veracruz/Altamira 8,240/8,180 vs. Panama Colón 7,140/6,880 per 20GP/40GP.

**Q3: Is a 40GP really cheaper than a 20GP?**
Yes, on both Mexican coasts this month: Pacific 40GP 5,100 vs. 20GP 5,250 (−150); Gulf 40GP 8,180 vs. 20GP 8,240 (−60). The inverted structure has held for multiple consecutive vintages — book 40ft boxes when volume allows, and remember 40HQ = 40GP price on the Pacific.

**Q4: How long does shipping from China to Mexico take?**
Third-party market references: 18–28 days port-to-port to the Pacific (as fast as 15–16 days from Tianjin on MSC's direct service since July 2026), 32–45 days to the Gulf via Panama; realistic door-to-door 35–47 days Pacific and 43–55 days Gulf including customs and inland delivery.

**Q5: What will I pay in Mexican duties and taxes on top of freight?**
Third-party policy facts: IGI per HS fraction (10–50% on 1,463 Chinese product categories under the Jan 1, 2026 reform; 185 more categories at 5–35% from April), IVA 16%, DTA 0.8% of customs value, plus broker fees. All-in DDP market quotes for a 40GP to Mexico City currently run about USD 6,500–8,500.

**Q6: Is LCL cheaper than FCL for small shipments from China to Mexico?**
Third-party market LCL rates are USD 78–208/CBM. Below roughly 15 CBM, LCL usually wins; at 30+ CBM a 40GP (5,100 published) beats LCL. Beware destination CFS fees — a 12-CBM LCL case study from third-party guides came out only 8% cheaper than FCL but 10 days slower.

**Q7: Which Mexico port should I choose on the Pacific coast — Manzanillo, Lázaro Cárdenas or Ensenada?**
The ocean freight is identical (5,250/5,100/5,100 published). Choose by inland logistics: Manzanillo for central/west consolidation, Lázaro Cárdenas for the fastest rail to Guadalajara/Mexico City, Ensenada for Baja California delivery.
